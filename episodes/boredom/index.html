Natural History of Your Attention

The Fallow Mind

New research is helping to reveal the ways we have been manipulated by our tech.

This tool has three levels of "explanation depth" — and an option to view through a neurotypical or ADHD lens.

Modern screens are built to make sure we never feel bored for even ten seconds. But like a garden needs a fallow season to replenish itself, our brains need some boredom to stay healthy and creative.

This tool explores what happens when we stop taking in constant information, and how a deeper network in the brain switches on—quietly stitching together memories, untangling stress, and sparking sudden insights.

So start reading to become bored. :-)

🌿 Recommended Reading & About fallow.garden

fallow.garden is an independent collection of small, single-purpose web tools built to push back against noisy feeds, constant tracking, and relentless self-optimization. Everything runs locally in your browser with no accounts and no telemetry.

Foundational Reading List on Boredom, Dopamine & The DMN

Dopamine Nation (2021) Dr. Anna Lembke — How high-dopamine digital inputs cause homeostatic receptor down-regulation, and how abstinence restores tonic balance.
Out of My Skull (2020) James Danckert & John D. Eastwood — The cognitive neuroscience of boredom as an evolutionary regulatory signal calling for purposeful engagement.
The Experience of Nature (1989) Rachel & Stephen Kaplan — The foundational text on Attention Restoration Theory (ART) and why "Soft Fascination" heals directed fatigue.
ADHD 2.0 (2021) Dr. Edward Hallowell & Dr. John Ratey — Explains the porous DMN-TPN switch in ADHD brains and how sensory/kinetic anchors prevent rumination.
Attention Span (2023) Dr. Gloria Mark — Empirical research on 47-second screen attention spans, switching costs, and the physiological toll of attention residue.
Reader, Come Home (2018) Maryanne Wolf — How rapid digital skimming rewires neural circuitry and starves the deep, contemplative pathways of the Default Mode Network.
