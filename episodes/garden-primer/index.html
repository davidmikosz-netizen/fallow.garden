Landscape & Living Ecology

Plants are not static green furniture. They are active biological agents that remember, choose, and communicate across both root systems and invisible atmospheric currents. This primer re-examines the living world around us—from the chemical breath of pines and oaks to our own role as wandering organisms learning how to become local once again.

🌿 About fallow.garden & References

fallow.garden is an independent anthology of single-purpose micro-utilities created to counter cognitive fatigue, algorithmic noise, and relentless optimization culture. Each tool runs entirely on your device with zero tracking or account friction.

Recommended Reading

  • Zoe Schlanger, The Light Eaters — Botanical intelligence, agency, and sensory navigation without a centralized brain.
  • Ed Yong, An Immense World & I Contain Multitudes — Sensory realms, evolutionary symbiosis, and non-human perspectives.
  • Peter Wohlleben, The Hidden Life of Trees — Forest communication, social lifelines, and subterranean fungal webs.
  • Merlin Sheldrake, Entangled Life — Fungal networks and the invisible chemical bridges connecting forest canopies.
  • Qing Li, Forest Bathing — The immunological and physiological impacts of biogenic volatile organic compounds (BVOCs).
  • Doug Tallamy, Bringing Nature Home — Restoring native trophic links and food webs in residential landscapes.
