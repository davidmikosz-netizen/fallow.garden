Field Guide to Digital Self-Defense

The Algorithmic Hook

When you play a physical card game at a kitchen table, the rules are honest: the deck is shuffled once, and the cards do not care whether you win, lose, or walk away. Modern phone and online games look like harmless pastimes, but under the hood they work very differently. By quietly tracking how long you pause, how many ads you tolerate, and what makes you frustrated, the game changes its own card draws and difficulty on the fly to keep you hooked. This guide explains how those hidden tricks work in plain English—with the option to dive as deep into the science as you like—because once you see the strings, the magic trick stops working.

🌿 Recommended Reading & About fallow.garden

fallow.garden is an independent collection of quiet, single-purpose web tools built to counter digital fatigue and attention-harvesting design. Every tool runs entirely on your device with zero tracking, zero ads, and no accounts.

Books & Research on How Games and Feeds Hook the Brain

Addiction by Design (2012) Natasha Dow Schüll — A fascinating look inside how digital slot machines and game algorithms are engineered to put players into a timeless "machine zone."
Dopamine Nation (2021) Dr. Anna Lembke — Explains why constant digital thrills tip our brain's pleasure-pain seesaw into restlessness, and how stepping back resets the balance.
The Molecule of More (2018) Daniel Z. Lieberman & Michael E. Long — Why dopamine is the chemical of "wanting and chasing" rather than feeling satisfied, and how to shift into the present moment.
The Craving Mind (2017) Dr. Judson Brewer — Shows how seeing a habit loop clearly ("disenchantment") breaks compulsive checking far more effectively than raw willpower.
EOMM: Engagement Matchmaking (2017) Chen et al. (Electronic Arts / UCLA) — The eye-opening industry paper showing how multiplayer games match players to prevent them from logging off rather than for fair play.
US Patent 9,789,406 (2017) Activision Publishing, Inc. — The actual patent describing how a game can match non-paying players against people with paid gear to nudge them into buying.
