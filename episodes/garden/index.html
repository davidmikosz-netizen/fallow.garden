The Analog Air Gap (The Paper Rule)

Never let an impulse break your focus or turn a restorative micro-break back into keyboard work. Keep a physical index card or notepad beside your workspace. When random stray thoughts, phantom tasks, or panic-replies strike, scribble them on paper. The tactile motor action physically quenches working-memory anxiety while keeping your eyes off the screen.

When to Deploy a Protocol

Attention fatigue rarely announces itself loudly—it shows up as micro-frictions, compulsive tab opening, and phantom urges. Step away for a protocol the moment you observe these behavioral signals:

Symptom 01 / Optical

The Glaze & Loop

Reading the exact same paragraph or email three times without retaining a single concept.

Symptom 02 / Habitual

The Phantom Check

Your hand automatically reaches for a phone or new browser tab without any conscious decision.

Symptom 03 / Latency

The Micro-Tab Spree

Opening secondary tabs simply because a primary page takes more than two seconds to load.

Symptom 04 / Cognitive

Friction Avoidance

A complex task becomes momentarily ambiguous, and you immediately flee into shallow administrative inbox checks.

Symptom 05 / Bandwidth

Attention Thrashing

Bouncing between chat, email, and documents while completing zero meaningful, coherent units of work.

Symptom 06 / Circadian

The Midday Dip

Your natural post-lunch biological trough hits, leaving your directed attention depleted and reactive.
